Pi 5 PoE NVMe Case

A slide-in tube for a Raspberry Pi 5 with the Waveshare PoE HAT (G) on its header and a Pimoroni NVMe Base Duo underneath. No screws or standoffs in either board: each board rests on its own ledge, the PCIe ribbon is the only link between them, and a click-in PLA lid at the SD end locates both against the closed USB end. The body prints standing on its USB end, so the ledges, vents and ports need no supports.

Print test, 11 Nov

  • Body: printed well (PLA, 10 Nov). The ledges hold the NVMe Base Duo; you shook the case and the base stayed put. No change to the body, so no reprint.
  • TPU lid: the snap bumps came out as faint nubs. The slicer did include them, but they were only 0.3–0.8 mm proud over five 0.3 mm layers, which TPU smoothed away. Even printed perfectly, only 0.55 mm would have reached into the windows.
  • v0.3 lid: PLA, with flexing latch arms (your suggestion). One arm per long side, cut free by slots and fixed at the top. Its 1 mm hook clicks into the upper side window that's already in your body. The toolpath now shows the full 1.0 mm hook over 12 layers. Print only the new lid.

Settled

  • SSDs: Samsung 980 series. Single-sided, 2.38 mm, no heatsink. On Pimoroni's 5 mm SSD standoffs they reach about 7.4 mm below the Duo, inside the 9.5 mm allowed.
  • HAT height: unknown, so kept at 16 mm. That's the tall case. If your HAT sits lower, you just get more air above it.
  • HAT stays on its headers. No hold-down ribs.
  • SD tray dropped. Fit the microSD card before the PCIe ribbon.

The print-ready STLs are in the chat: body (PETG), lid (PLA), four feet (TPU), DIN bracket (PETG). Each is already oriented for the bed.

Assembly
  1. Fit the SSDs to the Duo and the ribbon to both boards as Pimoroni describe, but leave the standoffs out. Plug the HAT onto the Pi's GPIO and PoE headers. Fit your microSD card now if you use one.
  2. Press four M3 × 5.7 heat-set inserts into the floor.
  3. Fold the Duo under the Pi and slide both in from the open end, USB first: the Duo onto the lower ledges, the Pi onto the upper ledges. Push them together by their SD edges until they stop on the end wall.
  4. Push the lid in until both latches click into the upper side windows. Its prongs sit against the boards' corners.
  5. Screw the TPU feet on with M3 × 8 socket heads.
  6. To open, pull the lid out by the thumb notch at the top. If it's tight, press both hooks in through the side windows with a small screwdriver as you pull.
Printing
  • Body: USB end flat on the bed, PETG. Every wall, the lip and the drive-bay walls are vertical in that orientation. Vent slots and windows only bridge 3–10 mm. No supports, no brim.
  • Lid: outer face on the bed, PLA (PETG also works and is more forgiving), 0.20 mm layers, no supports, no brim. The arm slots go right through the plate; check they're clear on the first layer. The hooks' undersides are 45° overhangs.
  • Feet: TPU, counterbore side up (no overhang). Turn them over to fit.
  • The click is set by latch_t (1.6, arm thickness) and latch_hook (1). Too stiff: thin the arm to 1.2. Too loose: raise the hook to 1.2. Either way, reprint only the lid (58m).
Hardware
  • 4 × M3 × 5.7 heat-set inserts, 4 × M3 × 8 socket head screws (feet)
  • DIN mount instead of feet: 4 × M3 × 20 socket head screws, TS35 rail (35 × 7.5 or 35 × 15)
  • Nothing for the boards. The Duo kit's standoffs and screws aren't used.

- main body
- lid

Make some sort of tray for the as card so it can be slotted in and out like a SIM card
