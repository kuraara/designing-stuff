Arcade Button Box

Self-contained wireless button boxes for long-barrel screw-in arcade buttons. Each box is a tub printed floor-down and a removable top plate printed face-down. The plate carries the buttons and both circuit boards, so you fit the nuts on the bench with full access, then drop the plate on and screw it down. The battery lives on the floor of a 30 mm rear bay. No supports anywhere.

Shared design notes

Why it's this big
ValueReason
50 pitch38 mm nut plus a 12 mm gap, enough for fingertips to spin a nut next to one already fitted and for wires to pass between switches.
28 edgeButton centre to inside wall: nut radius 19 plus 9 mm for wires running along the wall.
88 inside78 mm button depth − 4 mm plate + 14 mm below the tabs for straight .187" spades and a wire bend. Flag (right-angle) spades let you set conn_clear = 6 and lose 8 mm of height.
30 bay24 mm wide 21700 holder plus 6 mm. An 18650 holder (≈77 × 21) drops into the same fences.
95 min. length83 mm holder plus 6 mm each end for its leads. This is what makes the 1×1 wider than its button needs.
Ø29.0 hole1-1/8" (28.6) plus allowance, since FDM holes print slightly small. Check on the fit coupon.
12.5 × 7 portsSized for the USB-C cable overmold, not the plug, so the plug seats fully through the 2.4 mm wall.
Assembly order
  1. Print the fit coupon first (34m PLA). It has the button hole, a heat-set insert post, a countersunk M3, a USB-C port and the rocker cut-out.
  2. Press M3 × 5.7 heat-set inserts into the hanging corner posts (and centre or mid posts where the layout has them).
  3. Fit the buttons to the plate on the bench, spin the nuts down, clip the microswitches on.
  4. Put 1 mm foam tape on the pocket rails and press the TP4056 and ESP32 in component-side down, USB ends against the back stops. Solder wires straight to the pads; header pins would hit the plate.
  5. Wire the switches: COM tabs daisy-chained to GND, each NO tab to its GPIO.
  6. Set the battery holder on the floor rails between the corner fences. Strap it with a zip tie under the holder between the rails, or foam tape. Run its lead up to the TP4056 through a 2-pin JST-PH so the plate can come off.
  7. Snap the KCD11 rocker into the back wall from outside and wire it in series with TP4056 OUT+.
  8. Lower the plate. The lip locates it inside the walls. Fit the countersunk screws.
Electronics and pin map
cell ─ B+/B− ─[TP4056]─ OUT+ ─[rocker]─ 5V pin ─[ESP32-C3] └ OUT− ──────────────── GND OUT+ ─ 100k ─┬─ 100k ─ GND (tap → GPIO4 for battery voltage)

The rocker sits after the charger, so the cell charges with the box switched off. Switch off for a full charge; the ESP32 load can stop the TP4056 from terminating cleanly. The Super Mini's 3.3 V regulator runs from the 5V pin down to roughly a 3.5 V cell, which covers most of the capacity. Remove its red power LED if you use deep sleep.

B1GPIO0Deep-sleep wake capable
B2GPIO1Wake capable
B3GPIO3Wake capable
B4GPIO5Wake capable
B5GPIO2Wake capable. Strapping pin, so don't hold B5 while resetting
B6GPIO6No deep-sleep wake; light sleep is fine
VBATGPIO4ADC1, via the divider above
LEDGPIO8Onboard blue LED

Buttons use internal pull-ups and switch to GND. Only GPIO0–5 can wake the C3 from deep sleep, and only GPIO0–4 have the reliable ADC, which is why six buttons plus battery sensing lands like this.

Printing
  • Times are PrusaSlicer 2.7 estimates using the stock Original Prusa i3 MK3S & MK3S+ profile, 0.30 mm DRAFT (2 perimeters, 15 % grid), Generic PLA and Generic PETG. No supports, no brim.
  • Tub: floor on the bed. The corner posts hang from the rim with a 45° underside, and the port holes bridge 12.5–15 mm.
  • Plate: top face on the bed (a textured sheet gives a good top). The lip, board pockets and countersinks all print without support.
  • Height is the main cost: the tub is 90 mm tall because the buttons are. Dropping wall and floor_t from 2.4 to 1.6 cut the 2×2 tub from 6h 43m to 5h 38m in PLA.
  • Fit coupon: 34m PLA / 38m PETG. TPU feet (set of 4, Ø12 × 3, into 1 mm recesses): 16m.
  • PETG copes better with a hot car or a sunny desk. PLA is fine indoors.
Hardware per box
  • 4–6 × M3 × 8 countersunk screws and M3 × 5.7 heat-set inserts (4 on 1×1 to 1×3, 5 on 2×2, 6 on 1×4 and 2×3)
  • ESP32-C3 Super Mini, TP4056 USB-C module with protection, KCD11 mini rocker (15 × 10 cut-out)
  • 1-cell 21700 or 18650 holder with leads, JST-PH 2-pin pair
  • .187" female spades (2 per button), 24–26 AWG wire, 1 mm foam tape
  • 4 × Ø12 feet (printed TPU or stick-on)
Measure before printing
  • Button depth: from the underside of the bezel to the tips of the microswitch tabs. The design assumes 78 mm. Every millimetre here is a millimetre of box height (btn_depth).
  • Nut diameter: assumed 38 mm (btn_nut_d). A bigger nut means a bigger pitch.
  • Your spade type: straight or flag (conn_clear 14 or 6).
  • Board sizes: TP4056 modules vary between about 26 and 28 mm long.

Two cells: set cells = 2. The bay grows to 52 mm and every box gets 22 mm deeper.
